# CV / Resume Quality Criteria

Use these criteria as the internal review standard. They are guidance, not external evidence.

## Core principle

A strong CV or resume lets a reader quickly judge fit, strengths, evidence, and direction for the target opportunity. Evaluate the document according to its purpose: a resume should be concise, role-specific, easy to scan, and achievement-focused; a CV may include broader academic, research, project, teaching, award, and activity history.

## Review criteria

1. Target fit: relevant experiences are prioritized and irrelevant detail is reduced.
2. Evidence: claims are supported by concrete actions, methods, scope, tools, and results. Prefer facts and numbers over adjectives such as passionate or hardworking.
3. Narrative: education, projects, work, research, and activities form a coherent direction toward the target role.
4. Context: unfamiliar awards, acronyms, organizations, local programs, and internal project names are explained.
5. Structure: sections, dates, bullets, and ordering are consistent and easy to scan; unnecessary personal information and long paragraphs are avoided.
6. Bullet quality: prefer Action + Method + Scope + Result, without inventing outcomes that are absent from metadata.

## Scoring rubric

Score out of 100: target relevance 25, evidence and quantification 25, clarity and readability 15, strength positioning 15, narrative coherence 10, context and objectivity 5, completeness 5.

## Required feedback

Return strengths, critical issues, missing evidence, section-level feedback, concrete bullet rewrite suggestions based only on supplied facts, the score, and prioritized next actions.
